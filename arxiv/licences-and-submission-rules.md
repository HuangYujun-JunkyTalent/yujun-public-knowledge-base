# Select the most restrictive arXiv licence unless an explicit reason calls for another, because a licence once chosen cannot be changed

arXiv offers six licences.
From the most restrictive to the one that allows the most, they are the following.

1. arXiv.org perpetual, non-exclusive license 1.0, which lets arXiv distribute the paper and gives nobody else a right to reuse it.
2. CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.
3. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
4. CC BY-SA 4.0.
5. CC BY 4.0.
6. CC0 1.0.

The conditions `BY`, `NC`, `SA` and `ND` of the Creative Commons licences and the dedication `CC0` are as knowledge item `public-knowledge-base/licences/creative-commons.md` defines them.
Each step down the list allows more reuse.

Select the first licence by default.
Change to another licence only for an explicit reason, such as a funder or a publisher that requires a Creative Commons licence.
The licence chosen for a version is irrevocable and cannot be changed.
A later version may carry another licence.
The rights that an earlier version granted under a Creative Commons licence stay granted, because such a licence is irrevocable, as knowledge item `public-knowledge-base/licences/creative-commons.md` says.
A restrictive licence therefore can be loosened in a later version, and a permissive one can never be taken back.
The licence of arXiv also suits a later transfer of the copyright to a publisher, and CC0 leaves no copyright to transfer, as knowledge item `public-knowledge-base/licences/publishers-and-preprints.md` says.
The submitter certifies the right to grant the licence.
The title, the authors and the abstract are under CC0 1.0 in every case.

## Other rules of an arXiv submission

- The title is written in normal case and in ASCII characters.
- The authors are written as "Firstname Lastname", separated by commas, with the affiliations in parentheses.
- The abstract has at most 1920 characters.
- The comments field may hold the counts of pages and figures, and the status of the publication.
- The journal reference is entered only once the paper has appeared in a journal or a proceedings.
  The DOI field holds the DOI of such a published version, and the DOI that arXiv assigns stays out of it.
- A corrected or revised paper replaces the original submission as a new version, and is never a new submission.
- An announced paper cannot be removed.
  A withdrawal adds a version marked as withdrawn, and the earlier versions stay readable in full.
- The cutoff for submissions is 14:00 US Eastern time on each weekday, and a submission is announced at 20:00 of the first announcement day after its cutoff.
  arXiv announces from Sunday to Thursday, so a submission made between Thursday 14:00 and Friday 14:00 is announced on Sunday.
  The identifier is assigned only at the announcement.
- A new user, or a submission to a new category, may need an endorsement.
- arXiv compiles the source and shows the PDF to the submitter before the submission is complete, with its own TeX Live, as knowledge item `public-knowledge-base/latex/arxiv-tex-live.md` says.
- The publisher of the paper decides which version may stand on arXiv after the acceptance and the publication, as knowledge item `public-knowledge-base/licences/publishers-and-preprints.md` says for IEEE, ACM, Springer Nature and Elsevier.
