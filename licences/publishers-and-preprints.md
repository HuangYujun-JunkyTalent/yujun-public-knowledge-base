# A publisher takes the copyright of a paper or a licence to publish it, and each publisher sets which version an author may post and under which licence

A paper reaches a publisher under one of two agreements.
A transfer of copyright makes the publisher the copyright holder, and the author keeps only the rights that the agreement lists.
A licence to publish leaves the copyright with the author and gives the publisher the right to publish.
An open access paper carries a Creative Commons licence, usually `CC BY 4.0`, and the author keeps the copyright.

A paper exists in three versions.
The preprint is the version before peer review.
The accepted manuscript is the version after peer review, before the typesetting of the publisher.
The version of record is the version that the publisher typesets and publishes.

The table gives the policies of four publishers for a paper that is not open access.

| Publisher | Agreement | Preprint | Accepted manuscript | Version of record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IEEE | transfer of copyright, or `CC BY` or `CC BY-NC-ND` for an open access paper with a fee | on arXiv at any time, with the steps after acceptance below | in a thesis without permission, with the citation and the notice of IEEE | never posted |
| ACM | since 2026-01-01 every paper is open access under `CC BY` by default, the author keeps the copyright and grants a non-exclusive licence to publish | posted freely | posted freely | open access |
| Springer Nature | transfer of copyright, or open access under `CC BY` | on arXiv at any time, under any licence, Creative Commons included | on the personal website of the author at once, in a repository after an embargo of 12 months, or 6 months for Nature journals, never under a Creative Commons licence | never posted |
| Elsevier | transfer of copyright, or open access under `CC BY` | anywhere at any time | on arXiv or a personal page at once, under `CC BY-NC-ND 4.0`, with a link to the DOI | never posted |

The arXiv licence of a preprint holds even after a transfer of copyright, because the licence granted before the transfer is irrevocable.
A preprint under `CC0` leaves no copyright to transfer, which conflicts with the agreements of many publishers.
The non-exclusive licence of arXiv conflicts with the policies of none of the four publishers.

An author who reuses a figure of an own paper published under a transfer of copyright reuses a work of the publisher.
IEEE allows such reuse in a thesis without permission, with the citation and the copyright line `© 20XX IEEE`.
Other publishers state the reuse that they allow in their agreement.

## IEEE asks for two changes of the arXiv version after acceptance

An author may post a paper on arXiv before submitting it to IEEE, because a preprint is not a prior publication.
Once IEEE accepts the paper, the arXiv version carries the copyright notice of IEEE.
Once IEEE publishes the paper, the arXiv version is replaced either by the full citation with the DOI, or by the accepted manuscript with the copyright notice, the full citation and a link to IEEE Xplore.
