# Code or text published without a licence stays under exclusive copyright, so nobody may copy, change or share it

A creative work, code included, is under the exclusive copyright of its author from the moment it is made.
Publishing the work does not change the copyright.
Without a licence, others may read the work.
Copying, changing and redistributing it needs the permission of the author, apart from what an exception of copyright law allows, such as fair use or a quotation.

A public repository on GitHub grants one more right through the terms of service of GitHub.
Every user may view the repository and fork it through the forking function of GitHub.
The fork gives no right to use, change or share the code outside GitHub.

A repository that others are meant to reuse therefore carries a licence, as knowledge item `public-knowledge-base/licences/applying-a-licence.md` says.
A repository published for reading alone may stay unlicensed, and its author keeps every right.
