# State the result and leave out the rule it follows

A text that follows a rule does not say so.
A sentence that names a rule the writer followed, a check that passed or a permission taken as given tells the reader how the writer worked and nothing about the subject.
A reason that the text gives is a fact about the subject.
An agent's reply gives the result, and the record of what was done and why goes to the history of the ai-workspace.

> Rejected: *This session is recorded as a new history line in `history/<topic>/<line>.md`.*
> Replacement: the sentence is deleted, because the head of the line file records how the line started.

> Rejected: *Creating a README is an ask-for-permission, and I read your "feel free to implement" as consent.*
> Replacement: *The workspace had no entry point for a person or for an agent on another platform, so `README.md` now gives one.*

> Rejected: *The new prose passes the writing-rule scan, and every heading names its subject.*
> Replacement: the sentence is deleted.

A known violation is the exception.
When a text is delivered with a known violation of a rule, or without a procedure that matched the situation, the reply says so and gives the reason.
Without the sentence that reports the violation, the reader would take the text as compliant.
A text about a rule, such as a correction that a reviewer asked for, names the rule by its knowledge item.
