# The items of one formatted list are all sentences, all parts of one sentence, or all phrases, and each kind has its own case and ending

| The items are | First letter | End of each item |
|---|---|---|
| independent sentences | capital | period |
| parts of the sentence that the lead-in starts | lowercase | semicolon, and a period after the last item |
| names or phrases without a verb | lowercase | no mark |

Independent sentences:

> Take two steps before editing a file:
>
> - Read the README of its folder.
> - Find the knowledge items on the subject.

Parts of the sentence that the lead-in starts:

> Read the READMEs in each of the following situations:
>
> - at the start of a task;
> - before editing a file in a folder not yet visited.

Names or phrases without a verb:

> The connection needs the following software:
>
> - the local API of Zotero
> - the Better BibTeX extension

A proper noun and code keep their own case at the start of an item.
The last semicolon may be followed by "and" or "or".
No colon separates a verb or a preposition from the items that complete it.
An item that needs a sentence beside its phrase makes every item of the list a sentence.
An item may end with a colon when a nested list follows it.
Such an item keeps the colon whatever the pattern of its list.
The nested list may follow another pattern than the list that holds it.
A paper may keep the items of a list inside a sentence instead, one item per line in the source.
